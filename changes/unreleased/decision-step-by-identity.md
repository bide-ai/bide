`agent.SubmitDecision` names a decision's journal step by the decision (call, approver, verdict, scheme and decided_at), never by its signature bytes, so re-signing and resubmitting one decision concurrently with a randomized scheme (ECDSA P-256 from a KMS or HSM) journals one record instead of two, and `agent.ReportWritten` reports exactly one write; `agent.DecisionStep` returns the new name. Journals written under the old signature-derived names read, count and verify unchanged, a resubmission of such a decision writes nothing more, and a record that is not the approver's valid decision never takes the name the real decision needs (it is then journaled under the signature-derived name). An unchecked resubmission of a decision recorded under the old name is still a no-op that reports no write, and `agent.HeldDecisionStep` finds the record that holds a decision wherever it is (with a verifier it also reports whether the record verifies and can count) (`DecisionStep` is the name written first, not a lookup key). The approvals endpoint and `bide approve -to` find a decision's record by what it is rather than by its derived name, so a record under the fallback or an old name is returned as itself; the endpoint answers a request whose bytes equal a record that verifies under no current key and that the gate does not count as an invalid approval rather than a replay, and omits `receipt` from a replay answered from the recorded tally after a key rotation (`Response.Receipt` is now a pointer). `agent.IsDecisionStepName` checks the shape of a decision record name, and `bide approve -to` refuses a record name the endpoint returns that does not have it, and says when the receipt carries a different signature than the one sent.
