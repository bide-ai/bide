Section: Added

audit.AuditedStore.WithMinInterval and audit.AuditedEventStore.WithMinInterval publish at most one head per run or trail per interval, so an anchor that keeps every head for years (an S3 Object Lock bucket, say) no longer receives one per write: growth inside the interval is held back and published as one covering head by a timer, so while the process lives every record is covered within the interval, and a run's first head, its end marker (run:complete or run:aborted), Reanchor, Flush, ReanchorRuns and, for trails, ReanchorPending and AnchorTrails publish at once. A process that dies loses its timers, and what it held back stays unanchored until the next start or recovery pass: AuditedStore.ReanchorRuns is that pass for journals, and AnchorTrails already is for trails. The new AuditedStore.Flush and Pending, AuditedEventStore.Flush (which now also publishes held-back trails) and the default of no interval leave existing behaviour unchanged.
