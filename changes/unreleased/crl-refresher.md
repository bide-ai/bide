Section: Added

A CRL refresher for certificate-bound approvers, so a bank's platform team does not write its own CRL fetching loop. `audit.NewCRLRefresher(approver, sources, opts)` keeps the approver's `StatusSnapshot` supplied with the newest CRLs of the issuers the policy pins (`Run`, or `RefreshOnce` from cron): it fetches only from the URLs the policy file's `crl` member (or `audit.CRLSources`) names, over HTTP or HTTPS (LDAP is refused), with a size limit, timeouts, no compression and redirects only to the same host; verifies each CRL against its own issuer's certificate (the policy pins intermediates by key, so `intermediate_certs` supplies their certificates) before adding it; refuses a CRL with a lower number than the highest accepted (rollback) and a same-number CRL with another TBSCertList (equivocation); asks every configured URL and keeps the highest valid one; pairs delta CRLs with their base; archives every accepted CRL in a dated, atomically written `audit.DirCRLArchive` and rebuilds its rollback memory and the snapshot from it at start; and never makes status look fresher, because freshness is read only from each CRL's own dates. Events and alarms go to hooks, `Health()` reports the age and the next expected CRL, and the alarm is `min(max(limit/2, CAPeriod + overlap + tolerance), limit - interval)` with a `Critical` state that is exactly the status rule's own freshness test: a literal 50 percent of validity would alarm every cycle on a healthy weekly root, so the guide now advises a two-period validity for the root CRL with its `ExecStatusAgeFor` set to it. `bide identity fetch-crl` runs the same code for cron (`-once`) or as a daemon, `bide identity reset-high-water` and `ack-reset` are the audited way out of a CA's mistaken huge CRL number, and `bide approve -cert` still fetches nothing. The CRL URLs are not part of the policy digest because they cannot change a verdict.
