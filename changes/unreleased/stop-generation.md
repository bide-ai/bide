Section: Changed

A kill switch stop is now named by its scope, its compensation and a generation (`@stop/set/<scope key>/<generation>`, the generation in the signed record as `gen`) instead of by its content, and the insert is once-only, so two operators who set the same scope at the same instant can no longer leave it in force twice: the loser of the insert reads the control run again, finds the winner's stop in force, and writes nothing. `StopControl.SetStop` returns the stop in force with an `*AlreadyStoppedError` (`errors.Is(err, ErrAlreadyStopped)`) when its scope and compensation are already stopped, and `bide stop` reports "already stopped" and exits 0 as before. A writer with store access and no key cannot block a stop: only a set that decodes, validates and is named by its own content moves the generation (a junk entry under a generation name is stepped over and never counted, a number past 2^31-1 or not canonical is not a generation), and a writer that loses the insert eight times, or finds no generation free, writes the stop under its content-derived name, as every stop was before generations, which cannot be taken (such a stop can duplicate one written at the same moment, the safe direction). An upgrade that meets plain duplicate stops of one scope adopts the scope once. Records written before generations keep their old names and are read as before; the fold is unchanged. Model 16 gains the generation rule (`SetInsertable`, the invariant `OneActivePerScope`, the configs `stop-same-scope` and `stop-same-scope-reach` and the regress `no-generation`).
