Signing certificate-bound decisions from the command line, in the approver identity feature. `bide approve -cert chain.pem -identity-policy policy.json` (and `bide-hsm approve` with a key in a PKCS#11 token or a KMS) signs a decision over the `bide.approval.v4` bytes with the approver's own key and records it with the certificate chain, to a journal or through the approvals intake (`-to`). Before anything is signed it compares the key with the leaf certificate's key (the keys, not a signature), checks the certificate is valid now with at least `-cert-min-left` left, and checks the chain against the policy for `-as`; it reads the chain file before the HSM or KMS is opened, accepts only CERTIFICATE blocks (a private key block is refused unread, at most 8 certificates of 16 KiB), and never falls back to a bare-key decision. It refuses a bare-key decision by an approver the journal says is certificate-bound, and `-cert` by one it says is not; through the intake it checks the gate's identity rule from `GET /pending`, answers a 503 (revocation status not available yet) with a retry instruction and checks the returned receipt carries the chain and verifies under the leaf's key. New `audit.CertDecisionKey`. The browser approve page tells a certificate-bound approver to use `bide approve -to ... -cert`. The certificate-approvers guide gains a walkthrough from the CA's certificate to an approved call.
