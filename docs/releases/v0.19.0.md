**An approver can now be identified by a certificate from the institution's own CA, and a revocation stops a call from running.**

v0.19.0 adds certificate-bound approvers to approval gates. An approver id maps to an identity the bank's CA certifies instead of to a hand-enrolled public key; a decision counts only if its certificate chains to a trust anchor the policy names, carries the approver's identifier, and was valid and not revoked at a time the journal can bound; and the status is read again just before the call executes. Approvers with a bare key work as before. This is the first step of approver identity: bide verifies certificates and CRLs, it does not issue them, run SSO or claim the person was at the keyboard, and it is not a compliance certification. The release also adds `bide edit`, a read-only page for a flow config; carries the module set SBOM that v0.18.0 did not publish; and fixes the release script's wait on the Go module proxy.

- **Approvers identified by certificates.** A gate whose policy maps an approver id to an `audit.CertApprover` (the verifier behind `agent.CertDecisionVerifier`) counts a decision when it carries a chain and is signed over the new `bide.approval.v4` bytes (`agent.ApprovalDecisionBytesCert`, the v3 layout plus the SHA-256 of the leaf certificate), a signed head of the run covers it, the chain is accepted for that approver at the time the head fixes, and revocation status allows it. The seat is the verified identity, not the approver id: one canonical identifier fills one seat, one leaf key fills one seat, and `ExcludePrincipal` excludes a requester by identifier. A chain is accepted only through one path from the policy's trust anchors, with extended key usage nested along it, an algorithm and key profile, exactly one subject alternative name of the configured kind (an opaque person URI, or an email only by explicit choice), a required certificate policy and per-key `Distrust` dates. `audit.CertApprover.ValidateConfig` refuses a policy that cannot be met when the gate is configured (`agent.ErrConfig`), such as a CRL period longer than half the pre-execution age. <!-- PENDING E2b: the gate no longer refuses a certificate-bound approver at configuration; check the final wording and the name of the option that wires it. -->

- **Revocation is honoured when a decision is counted and again just before the call runs.** Status comes from CRLs (`audit.StatusSnapshot`, `CertIdentity.EvaluateStatus`): full CRLs and delta CRLs (merged with their base as RFC 5280 describes), checked against the issuing CA's key, refused when bide cannot read them without guessing (issuing distribution points, indirect CRLs, unprocessed critical extensions, SHA-1 signatures, a missing CRL number or `nextUpdate`). A certificate that was ever on hold is refused unless `StatusParams.AllowHolds`. A counted decision is checked again against status no older than `ExecStatusAge` immediately before the tool runs; if its chain now shows a revocation effective at or before the decision, or status is too old, the call does not run and the gate pauses with a named reason (a held call is re-asked or cancelled, never silently denied). Missing or stale status pauses the gate; it does not deny it. <!-- PENDING E2b: confirm the pause names (`awaiting-status`, hold generations) and that the exec check is on every path to the tool. -->

- **Two published latency tiers.** The time from a CA recording a revocation to a gate refusing to act on it is bounded by the CA's publication delay, its CRL (or delta) period and `ExecStatusAge`, and `ExecStatusAge` must be at least twice the CA period the policy declares (`CAPeriod`), so the operator computes the number and records it. It differs by tier. For the approver's own certificate it is minutes when the issuing CA publishes CRLs or deltas on a period of minutes. For a CA above it, status comes from the root's CRL, which is slower (`StatusParams.MaxStatusAgeFor` gives an offline root's weekly CRL its own age limit), so a revoked issuing CA can take days to a week to appear there; the fast path for a compromised issuing CA is a policy change, `IdentityPolicy.Distrust` for its key, which ends trust in it for decisions provable at or after the time given without voiding earlier history. bide does not hide the slower tier: a revocation the CA never published, or published only after the retention ended, is invisible.

- **Provisional and final.** Every certificate-bound decision is recorded with a tier. A decision is provisional when a later status could still change it (the window is short, a CRL series has a gap, or the count was made live), and final only when status reaches past the time the decision became provable plus the window and the series covers it; a live count is provisional at most. Only a final finding is verified, and its basis says what it rests on. A revocation that turns out to be effective before a counted decision is reported as a dispute by the audit, not silently rewritten: whether an executed action stands is the bank's process.

- **What the journal records.** Before the tally, the gate journals the CRLs the count rests on by hash (`approval-status:<sha256>`; one above 256 KiB goes to `CertApprover.StatusArchive`) and an `approval-identity:<id>:<generation>` record: the policy in force with every parameter that affects the verdict, the roster digest, and for each counted decision its chain hashes, anchor, covering head, time source, tier, basis and status hashes, bound to the tally by a digest of its bytes. A decision with no covering head makes the gate ask the store to publish one (`Reanchorer`, at most once per ten seconds per run), and only for a decision whose chain, identifier, scheme and signature already verify, so a junk decision never makes the gate sign. A gate with a certificate-bound approver records its terminal tally as `approval-tally:<id>:ident`; a plain tally that passed is superseded and the gate asks again, and a valid hold before execution supersedes the passed tally and the next count is `approval-tally:<id>:ident-reask:<g+1>`. <!-- PENDING E2c #350: confirm names and the PendingIdentReapproval and PendingExecHoldReapproval reasons. -->

- **The identity is shown, with its basis.** `bide view`'s run page and Needs attention list, and `bide approve -list`, show the identifier, the certificate's display name (marked as not part of the identity), the tier and basis, the status time and its age, the time the decision became provable and its source, the window, the generation and the policy digest, "as recorded at counting by the gate, not re-verified here". Nothing is drawn as verified without an audit result the viewer is given: the journal alone gives "final as recorded", a provisional count is amber and hatched, and a run is VERIFIED only when no gate is in a provisional, held, incomplete or changed-policy state. `bide approve -verify-receipt` and `bide audit verify-receipts` take `-identity-policy`: a receipt that carries a chain is checked against the anchor time of its own anchoring head, and the output says that revocation status was not checked. <!-- PENDING E3 #341: confirm the flags and wording, then add the images below (they come from assets/demo/shots-identity.sh). -->

- **Checked against real CAs.** Conformance captures of two-tier approver PKIs with true delta CRLs, from EJBCA Community 9.6.3 (an RSA-3072 root and an ECDSA P-256 issuing CA) and Microsoft AD CS on Windows Server 2025 (an RSA-3072 standalone root and subordinate on two machines), are checked in under `audit/testdata/ejbca` and `audit/testdata/adcs` and run offline through the chain and status rules, along with OpenSSL-written certificates and CRLs (`audit/testdata/openssl`). They cover base and delta pairing, cumulative deltas, key compromise, supersession and hold release. Two CA behaviours are declared, never assumed: `IdentityPolicy.NotBeforeBackdate` (AD CS back-dates `notBefore` by about ten minutes, so a 24 hour certificate spans 24 hours and ten minutes) and `StatusParams.ThisUpdateSkew` (the same for `thisUpdate`). The capture workflow is `crl-fixtures.yml`, run by hand. The captures are two products on specific versions, not a statement about every deployment of either.

- **Formal models.** Model 15 (`spec/tla/identity`) checks the counting rule (identity bound, revocation sound, fail closed, no backdating, one seat per person and per identifier, terminal stability, no execution after a known revocation, a final verdict only over a complete series) with a counterexample configuration for each rule that switches it off, and a CA tier: the leaf's issuing CA is certified by a root with its own, slower CRL series that may be withheld, with the count and the pre-execution check reading each tier at its own age. Model 1b gains a certificate-bound approver, the status check on every path to the tool (with crashes, redeploys and recovery), the re-ask that lets a replacement decision execute after a revocation held the call, and the decision relation. `audit.TestModel15Refinement` runs each counterexample configuration of model 15 as a scenario through `agent.TallyApprovals` and `audit.CertApprover` and compares the counted set with the model's. `modelsync` holds the identity functions to the model's map. <!-- PENDING M2 #349 and E2b: the refinement test lists three pre-execution configurations as pending until the status check before execution lands; confirm they are covered, and the scenario count (16 at #349). -->

- **`bide edit`, a read-only page for a flow config.** `bide edit <file.yaml|.yml|.json>` serves a local page on a loopback address that shows a flow config as `bide check` and `bide view -config` read it: the verdict, the declared graph, the nodes and wiring in config order, the selected node's block, safety, crash behaviour and approval gate, the assertions with their verdicts and counterexamples, and the source. All of it is computed by the code the command line runs, and the page labels the check as structural. It does not edit anything yet. The server refuses a non-loopback `Host`, takes API calls only as JSON from its own origin with a per-run token, and sends a strict content security policy. The new module `flowedit` carries the engine the editing will use: `flowedit.Apply` changes one value, entry or item by splicing, so every byte outside the edit is the original byte (comments, key order and blank lines survive), and returns text only after `Verify` re-parses it and checks it is the old text with the operation applied; anything it cannot splice safely is a `*Refusal` and the text is untouched. <!-- PENDING #340: confirm it merged, the command name and the module's stability label (experimental). Behaviour changes:

- **Nothing changes for a gate whose approvers have bare keys.** Certificate-bound approvers are opt-in through the policy. A bare-key verifier never counts a `bide.approval.v4` signature, and `WithDecisionCheck`, which checks a decision as it is submitted, refuses a decision that carries a chain unless a certificate-bound verifier is configured for it. <!-- PENDING E2b: confirm the second half against the merged SubmitDecision. --> A receipt that carries a chain is refused by `DecisionReceipt.Verify` (`audit.ErrCertificateBound`); use `VerifyCertificate`.
- **A gate with a certificate-bound approver can pause where a bare-key gate would not.** It waits for a covering signed head (and asks the store to publish one), and for status newer than its age limits; it names the reason in Needs attention and the notifier. A store that cannot publish a head, or a CA whose CRLs are older than the policy allows, stops such a gate from passing; it does not weaken it.
- **A gate that has counted under one kind of approver does not count under the other.** Whether a gate is certificate-bound is read from its journal as well as from the policy, so a downgraded or upgraded policy cannot count a bound gate with bare keys, or the reverse: a passed plain tally is superseded and the gate asks again. <!-- PENDING E2c #350: confirm. -->
- **The status rule is strict about what it cannot read.** A CRL with an entry dated more than 30 minutes after its own `nextUpdate`, an issuing distribution point, an indirect entry, a critical extension bide does not process or a SHA-1 signature makes the status unknown, and a negative or over-cap `ThisUpdateSkew` or `MaxStatusAgeFor` does too. Status unknown never counts a decision.
- **Models.** `modelsync` now holds `audit` `VerifyChain`, `EvaluateStatus`, `AuditedStore.Reanchor`, `CertApprover.VerifyCertDecision` and `agent.TallyApprovals` to model 15's map; a change to one without the model fails the check.
