**An approver can decide with a certificate from the institution's own CA, and a revocation stops the call from running.**

v0.19.0 adds certificate-bound approvers to approval gates, end to end. The approver makes a key and a certificate request (`bide keygen -csr`, or the HSM or KMS vendor's tooling), the bank's CA issues a certificate, and the approver decides with `bide approve -cert` (or `bide-hsm approve -cert` for a key in an HSM or KMS), straight to the journal or through the application's approval intake. A decision counts only if its certificate chains to a trust anchor the policy names, carries the approver's identifier, and was valid and not revoked at a time the journal can bound; revocation status is read when the decision is counted and again just before the call runs. Approvers with a bare key work as before. bide verifies certificates and CRLs, it does not issue them, run SSO or claim the person was at the keyboard, and this is not a compliance certification. The release also adds `bide edit`, a read-only page for a flow config; carries the module set SBOM that v0.18.0 did not publish; and fixes the release script's wait on the Go module proxy.

What is not there yet, stated plainly. bide does not fetch CRLs: the application adds the CRLs it fetches to the status snapshot. The browser approve page cannot hold a certificate and tells a certificate-bound approver to use `bide approve -to ... -cert`. The independent-time tier of a final verdict has no time source (the RFC 3161 verifier from v0.18.0 is still not called by anything), so a final verdict rests on the log signer's clock. `bide-hsm csr`, which would build the request for an HSM or KMS key, is not written: the guide has the procedures that were run for PKCS#11 and links the vendors' for KMS, and AWS KMS has none.

- **From the CA's certificate to an approved call.** The [operator guide](https://github.com/bide-ai/bide/blob/v0.19.0/docs/guides/certificate-approvers.md) walks one approver through it. The platform team writes the certificate policy file (trust anchors, pinned intermediates, the approver-to-identifier map, the EKU and policy OID the CA asserts) and configures the application's gate. The approver runs `bide keygen -out ops.key -as ops -csr ops.csr.pem`, which writes a subject-less PKCS#10 request for an Ed25519 or ECDSA P-256 key (never overwriting, never printing the key). For a key in a PKCS#11 token the guide shows the request made with GnuTLS `certtool` (run against SoftHSM 2.7.0 for one P-256 and one Ed25519 key, with the `bide-hsm pubkey` comparison to check the request was made by the key `bide-hsm approve` will sign with); for AWS KMS, Google Cloud KMS and Azure Key Vault it links the vendors' procedures, which were not run. The CA issues the certificate after its own identification of the person, with one subject alternative name that is the identifier. The approver assembles the chain (leaf first, no anchor, at most 8 certificates) and runs `bide approve -db app.db -run refund-1049 -call c2 -as ops -key ops.key -cert chain.pem -identity-policy policy.json`, or adds `-to https://approvals.bank.example`. Before it signs anything, the command compares the key with the leaf's key, checks the certificate is valid now with at least `-cert-min-left` left, and checks the chain against the policy for `-as`; it accepts only CERTIFICATE blocks, never falls back to a bare-key decision, and refuses `-cert` for an approver the journal says is bare-key and the reverse.

- **Taking the decision over HTTP, checked before it is written.** `agent.WithDecisionCheck` and `agent.SubmitDecision` now verify a decision that carries a chain through the approver's verifier (`agent.CertIntakeVerifier`, implemented by `audit.CertApprover.VerifyCertIntake`: the chain accepted for the approver at the time of the write, the `bide.approval.v4` signature under the leaf's key, status usable now) instead of refusing it, and write nothing on any failure. The approvals intake takes the chain in the request (`cert`, a base64 DER array, leaf first, bounded before any parsing), answers 503 `status_unavailable` with `Retry-After` and writes nothing when status cannot be judged (`agent.ErrStatusUnavailable`; retry), answers 422 for an invalid or revoked chain or a downgrade (a certificate-bound approver without a chain, a bare-key approver with one), answers a replay of a held decision from the record even during an outage, and anchors after the write. `counted: true` on a certificate-bound decision is not final: each decision carries `tier`, `provisional` and `basis`, and the response carries `anchored`. `GET /pending` lists the identifier each certificate-bound approver's certificate must carry, which lists person identifiers, so restrict who can read a gate. Receipts carry the chain.

- **Approvers identified by certificates.** A gate whose approver resolver returns an `audit.CertApprover` (an `agent.CertDecisionVerifier`) counts a decision when it carries a chain and is signed over the new `bide.approval.v4` bytes (`agent.ApprovalDecisionBytesCert`, the v3 layout plus the SHA-256 of the leaf certificate), a signed head of the run covers it, the chain is accepted for that approver at the time the head fixes, and revocation status allows it. The seat is the verified identity, not the approver id: one canonical identifier fills one seat, one leaf key fills one seat, and `ExcludePrincipal` excludes a requester by identifier. A chain is accepted only through one path from the policy's trust anchors, with extended key usage nested along it, an algorithm and key profile, exactly one subject alternative name of the configured kind (an opaque person URI, or an email only by explicit choice), a required certificate policy and per-key `Distrust` times. `CertApprover.ValidateConfig` refuses a policy that cannot be met when the gate is built (`agent.ErrConfig`): a CRL period longer than half the pre-execution age, a pinned CA without its declared values, or a status snapshot with no bound or archive. The [operator guide](https://github.com/bide-ai/bide/blob/v0.19.0/docs/guides/certificate-approvers.md) covers what to declare from your CA, which is read from a capture of your own CA, and what to do when you revoke.

- **Revocation is honoured when a decision is counted and again just before the call runs.** Status comes from CRLs (`audit.StatusSnapshot`, `CertIdentity.EvaluateStatus`): full CRLs and delta CRLs (merged with their base as RFC 5280 describes), checked against the issuing CA's key, and refused when bide cannot read them without guessing (issuing distribution points, indirect CRLs, unprocessed critical extensions, SHA-1 signatures, a missing CRL number or `nextUpdate`). A certificate that was ever on hold is refused unless `StatusParams.AllowHolds`. Immediately before the tool runs (on every path to it: the first execution, a resume, crash recovery, `RecoverLoop`, a middleware retry, and a plan flow node before its body), every counted certificate-bound decision is checked again against status no older than `ExecStatusAge` for the leaf's issuing CA and its own `ExecStatusAgeFor` for each CA above it. A decision whose chain now shows a revocation effective at or before it, or that no longer verifies for good, writes an exec hold (`approval-exec-hold:<id>:<generation>`, `agent.ExecHold`, with the certificate serial, the effective time, the reason code and the CRL's hash) and the call does not run; the next generation is counted without it, so replacement decisions run the call (`agent.PendingExecHoldReapproval`). Status that is missing or stale, or a decision no head covers right now, only pauses the gate (`agent.PendingIdentityStatus`): nothing is written and no approval notification is sent, because nobody is asked to decide, and a drive has to run again (your own `Resume`, or `RecoverLoop`) after the fresh CRL is added.

- **Two published latency tiers.** The longest time from a CA recording a revocation to a gate refusing to run on it differs by tier, and the operator computes and publishes both (`audit.ExecLatencyBound`). For the approver's own certificate it is the CA's publication delay plus the issuing CA's CRL (or delta) period plus `ExecStatusAge`, plus any declared `ThisUpdateSkew`; `ExecStatusAge` must be at least twice the CA's period, so with a CA that publishes every 30 minutes, a one hour `ExecStatusAge` and a five minute publication delay the bound is 95 minutes. For a CA above it, status comes from the root's CRL, so the bound is the time to hold the root ceremony and publish, the root's CRL period, the declared overlap and that CA's `ExecStatusAgeFor`: for a root that publishes weekly it is days to a week or more. The fast path for a compromised issuing CA is a policy change, removing the intermediate from `IdentityPolicy.Inter` or setting `IdentityPolicy.Distrust` for its key (which ends trust in it for decisions provable at or after the time given, without voiding earlier history), and redeploying. Neither number covers the instant between the check and the call beginning, and a revocation the CA never published, or published only after the retention ended, is invisible.

- **Provisional and final.** A gate counts a decision as soon as it is provable and its chain is accepted, with a status that is fresh, and that count is provisional: a revocation effective before the decision may still be reported after the CRL it used. A decision is final only when status reaches past the time it became provable plus the window (at least `MinW`) and the series is complete up to the first CRL beyond the certificate's `notAfter`; a live count is provisional at most. The record carries the tier (`provisional`, `logclock-final`; `independent-final` is defined for a time source independent of the log signer, which nothing supplies yet) and its basis, and a policy can require final before a call runs (`held-until-final`). Only an audit result is called verified. A revocation that turns out to be effective before a counted decision is reported by the audit as a dispute, not silently rewritten: whether an executed action stands is the bank's process.

- **What the journal records.** Before the tally, the gate journals the CRLs the count rests on by hash (`approval-status:<sha256>`; one above 256 KiB goes to `CertApprover.StatusArchive`) and an `approval-identity:<id>:<generation>` record: the policy in force with every parameter that affects the verdict, the roster digest, and for each counted decision its chain hashes, anchor, covering head, time source, tier, basis and status hashes, bound to the tally by a digest of its bytes. A decision that verifies but has no covering head makes the gate ask the store to publish one (`Reanchorer`, at most once per ten seconds per run), and only for a decision whose chain, identifier, scheme and signature already verify, so a junk decision never makes the gate sign; pause notes name the phase (`awaiting-anchor`, `awaiting-status`, `held-until-final`, `exec-hold`). A gate with a certificate-bound approver records its terminal tally as `approval-tally:<id>:ident` (`agent.ApprovalIdentTallyStep`); a plain tally that passed is superseded and the gate asks again (`agent.PendingIdentReapproval`), and a valid exec hold of generation g supersedes the passed generation-g tally, the next count being `approval-tally:<id>:ident-reask:<g+1>`. The notifier, `agent.EvaluateGate`, `plan` `Conform`, `audit.ApprovalEvidence` and `VerifyApprovals` read this chain by one rule.

- **The identity is shown, with its basis.** `bide view` (the run page, the Needs attention list and the JSON view) and `bide approve -list` show, for a gate with a certificate-bound approver, the verified identifier, the certificate's display name (marked as not part of the identity), the tier and basis, the status time, when the decision became provable and its time source; a gate paused for status shows as "paused: identity status unknown" and a call held before execution is listed. Nothing is drawn green or called verified without an audit result the viewer is given: the journal alone gives "final as recorded" in a neutral style, and provisional, recorded-but-not-counted, held, incomplete and policy-mismatched identities are each visibly non-final, so a finished run reads COMPLETED or IDENTITY NOT FINAL, never VERIFIED, on the gate's word; a dispute is shown only from an audit result. For a pending certificate-bound approver, `bide approve -list` and the Needs attention list print the `bide approve -cert` command with placeholders, and offer the bare-key command only for the bare-key approvers, since a bare-key decision for a bound approver would be recorded and never counted. `bide approve -verify-receipt` (with `-identity-policy` and `-log-key`) and `bide audit verify-receipts` (with `-identity-policy`) check a certificate-bound receipt against a certificate policy file at the time the decision became provable (`audit.ParseIdentityPolicy`, `DecisionReceipt.VerifyIdentity`), and say that revocation status was not checked.

- **Checked against real CAs.** Conformance captures of two-tier approver PKIs with true delta CRLs, from EJBCA Community 9.6.3 (an RSA-3072 root and an ECDSA P-256 issuing CA) and Microsoft AD CS on Windows Server 2025 (an RSA-3072 standalone root and subordinate on two machines), are checked in under `audit/testdata/ejbca` and `audit/testdata/adcs` and run offline through the chain and status rules, along with OpenSSL-written certificates and CRLs (`audit/testdata/openssl`). They cover base and delta pairing, cumulative deltas, key compromise, supersession and hold release. Two CA behaviours are declared, never assumed: `IdentityPolicy.NotBeforeBackdate` (AD CS back-dates `notBefore` by about ten minutes, so a 24 hour certificate spans 24 hours and ten minutes) and `StatusParams.ThisUpdateSkew` (the same for `thisUpdate`). The capture workflow is `crl-fixtures.yml`, run by hand. The captures are two products on specific versions, not a statement about every deployment of either.

- **Formal models.** Model 15 (`spec/tla/identity`) checks the counting rule (identity bound, revocation sound, fail closed, no backdating, one seat per person and per identifier, terminal stability, no execution after a known revocation, a final verdict only over a complete series) with a counterexample configuration for each rule that switches it off, and a CA tier: the leaf's issuing CA is certified by a root with its own, slower CRL series that may be withheld, with the count and the pre-execution check reading each tier at its own age. Model 1b gains a certificate-bound approver, the status check on every path to the tool (with crashes, redeploys and recovery), the re-ask that lets a replacement decision execute after a revocation held the call, and the decision relation. `audit.TestModel15Refinement` runs each counterexample configuration of model 15 as a scenario through `agent.TallyApprovals` and `audit.CertApprover` and compares the counted set with the model's, with a positive control for each (the same setup with the rule satisfied), and the six pre-execution configurations run through the gate's one pre-execution path and the verifier's check, which must agree; a drift guard fails when a configuration has no scenario. `modelsync` holds the identity functions to the model's map.

- **`bide edit`, a read-only page for a flow config.** `bide edit <file.yaml|.yml|.json>` serves a local page on a loopback address that shows a flow config as `bide check` and `bide view -config` read it: the verdict, the declared graph, the nodes and wiring in config order, the selected node's block, safety, crash behaviour and approval gate, the assertions with their verdicts and counterexamples, and the source. All of it is computed by the code the command line runs, and the page labels the check as structural. It does not edit anything yet. The server refuses a non-loopback `Host`, takes API calls only as JSON from its own origin with a per-run token, and sends a strict content security policy. The new module `flowedit` carries the engine the editing will use: `flowedit.Apply` changes one value, entry or item by splicing, so every byte outside the edit is the original byte (comments, key order and blank lines survive), and returns text only after `Verify` re-parses it and checks it is the old text with the operation applied; anything it cannot splice safely is a `*Refusal` and the text is untouched.

- **Release script and SBOM.** v0.18.0 did not publish its module set SBOM, because `scripts/module-sbom.sh` wrote the file relative to the temporary module it changes into; that is fixed, with a self-test, and `bide_0.19.0_modules.sbom.json` (with its `.sigstore.json`) is published and signed with this release. `scripts/release.sh` no longer asks the Go module proxy about a new tag until the tag can be fetched directly and 300 seconds have passed, then waits with growing intervals for up to 90 minutes, because the proxy caches a miss for 30 to 60 minutes and polling kept it fresh (every stage of the v0.18.0 release failed that way).

- **Fixes.** The `store/sqlite` write-fairness tests no longer depend on goroutine scheduling and no longer fail on a loaded machine (test change only). `docs/ROADMAP.md` lists only open work.

Behaviour changes:

- **Nothing changes for a gate whose approvers have bare keys.** Certificate-bound approvers are opt-in through the approver resolver, and `WithDecisionCheck` and `SubmitDecision` behave as before for a bare-key decision. A decision that carries a chain is now verified through the approver's certificate verifier and refused (`agent.ErrInvalidApproval`, 422 on the intake) when the approver is bare-key or the chain is not accepted; before, `WithDecisionCheck` refused every such decision. A bare-key verifier never counts a `bide.approval.v4` signature, `DecisionReceipt.Verify` refuses a receipt that carries one (`audit.ErrCertificateBound`); use `VerifyCertificate` or `VerifyIdentity`.
- **A gate with a certificate-bound approver can pause where a bare-key gate would not.** It waits for a covering signed head (and asks the store to publish one) and for status newer than its age limits, and a call can be held at the last step before it runs. A store that cannot publish a head, or a CA whose CRLs are older than the policy allows, stops such a gate from passing; it does not weaken it. A pause for status sends no notification: watch `agent.LatestPausePhase` and your CRL ages, and re-drive the run after adding a fresh CRL, since nothing in the gate retries by itself.
- **A gate that has counted under one kind of approver is not counted under the other.** Whether a gate is certificate-bound is read from its journal (an identity tally, an exec hold, or a note marked `identity_bound`) as well as from the policy. A policy that lost the certificate-bound approvers or changed their kinds is refused with `ErrConfig`, a plain tally that passed on a bound gate is superseded and the gate asks again, a bound count that passed without excluding the requester refuses with `ErrConfig` if the policy then excludes it, and the dead end of separation of duties turned on after a bound count wraps `agent.ErrGateNeedsNewRun`. A plan flow gate no longer takes a pass from a plain-name tally.
- **The status rule is strict about what it cannot read.** A CRL with an entry dated more than 30 minutes after its own `nextUpdate`, an issuing distribution point, an indirect entry, a critical extension bide does not process or a SHA-1 signature makes status unknown, as does a negative or over-cap `ThisUpdateSkew` or `MaxStatusAgeFor`. Unknown status never counts a decision.
- **`modelsync` holds more functions to the model.** `audit` `VerifyChain`, `EvaluateStatus`, `AuditedStore.Reanchor`, `CertApprover.VerifyCertDecision` and `agent.TallyApprovals` are held to model 15's map: a change to one without the model fails the check.

Upgrading:

- Finish or resolve runs before upgrading, as with every pre-release.
- Roll the whole fleet to v0.19.0 before enabling a certificate-bound approver, and remove the old bare key from every resolver at the switch: a process without this change cannot refuse to count a stolen bare signature.
- The approvals intake changes for an application that already mounts it: a decision that carries `cert` is verified before it is written, and `GET /pending` gains `gate.identity` for a certificate-bound gate. A gate with only bare-key approvers sees no change.
- To adopt certificate-bound approvers, follow [Operating certificate-bound approvers](https://github.com/bide-ai/bide/blob/v0.19.0/docs/guides/certificate-approvers.md): capture your own CA, declare its CRL period and the other facts it lists, set `ExecStatusAge` to at least twice the period, and compute and publish both revocation latency bounds.
- An organization that hosts the approve page checks the files it serves against the release's signed `approve-page.SHA256SUMS` before each deploy, as before.
